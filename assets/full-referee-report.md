# Full referee report scaffold — internal heading, never print

Use only after the proposed report plan has been confirmed. Delete all bracketed instructions and unused optional sections. The delivered author-facing report must contain only polished report prose.

## Default: standard report

# Referee Report: “[Manuscript title]”

*Manuscript: [ID], [Journal]* [Omit this line when the metadata is unavailable or the submission system supplies it.]

## 1. Summary

[In one to three neutral paragraphs, reconstruct the research question, model or empirical design, closest benchmark, main results, and evidence. Select the facts needed for the later evaluation; do not copy the abstract or insert criticism prematurely.]

## 2. Overall Evaluation

[Begin with the strongest specific asset: problem importance, formulation, theorem, method, data, or insight. Then state one central reservation and explain why it controls the journal-level assessment. Calibrate the revision scope. Include an explicit recommendation here only when the confirmed plan makes it author-facing.]

## 3. Major Comments

### 1. [A complete, conclusion-bearing diagnostic title]

[Write two to five connected paragraphs, not a labeled audit form. Accurately reconstruct the relevant claim and cite the manuscript anchor. Introduce the decisive comparison, counterfactual, omitted first-order mechanism, derivation, or evidence. Explain why it changes novelty, scope, validity, interpretation, or usefulness. End with a proportionate request or state what evidence/result/repositioning could change the assessment.]

### 2. [A complete, conclusion-bearing diagnostic title]

[Repeat only for a distinct root issue.]

## Other Comments

[Optional. Use a short numbered list for localized definitions, exposition, robustness, reproducibility, citations, or corrections that remain worth the authors' time. Omit this section when the decision is controlled entirely by a few paper-spine issues.]

## 4. Conclusion

[Optional. Synthesize the strongest asset, decisive bottleneck, and realistic publication or revision path without introducing a new concern or repeating every major comment. Put the recommendation here only if the confirmed plan calls for it and it was not already stated.]

## References

[Optional. Include only external works actually discussed in the report, after verifying their bibliographic details and relevance.]

## Structural variants

- **Concise:** title/ID → Summary and Overall Assessment → Major Concerns → Minor Comments. Use for short reports or when the review form separately captures the recommendation.
- **Standard:** use the numbered structure above. This is the default and best matches a conventional full referee report.
- **Issue-led:** title/ID → short opening assessment → substantive thematic headings → optional conclusion. Use when two or three long, paper-spine arguments are clearer than a separate Major Comments container.
- **Dimension-led narrative:** title/ID → neutral Summary → Evaluation organized under Motivation and Scope, Model and Analysis, Technical Concerns, and Presentation → final Overall Summary. Use for compact reports whose concerns are clearer by assessment dimension than by numbered issue; omit any empty dimension.

Do not switch to an AE-style synthesis unless the confirmed role is AE. An AE report may summarize reviewer positions and the decision path; a referee report should present its own analysis.

## Hidden completeness check — never print these labels

Before finalizing each major comment, verify internally that it has enough of the following chain:

`accurate manuscript anchor → decisive comparison or test → lower-level evidence → upper-level consequence → proportionate author request`

The chain need not occupy five separate paragraphs, and a strong rejection comment need not invent a locally feasible repair. Do not print internal fields such as paper-level gate, necessary subclaim, severity, confidence, evidence role, repair scope, status, or disconfirmation.

Also verify:

- the opening summary demonstrates understanding and does not strategically omit the manuscript's strongest result;
- the overall evaluation gives a balanced but decisive publication thesis;
- major comments are ordered by editorial importance and share no avoidable overlap;
- tentative concerns remain questions rather than being upgraded to facts;
- the conclusion adds no new objection;
- recommendation wording and visibility match the confirmed plan;
- verification limits appear only when material and then as natural prose, not a mandatory audit heading;
- confidential comments, if requested, are delivered separately and do not conceal answerable scientific objections from the authors.

## Document appearance when DOCX or PDF is requested

Use a restrained academic report layout or a user-supplied template: readable serif body text, clear bold headings, conventional margins, sparse numbering, and unobtrusive page numbers when useful. Color is optional, not a journal norm. Avoid tables, decorative panels, and dense nested bullets in the delivered report. Keep headings with the paragraph that follows. Render and inspect every page, including widow/orphan behavior and the final page, before delivery.
