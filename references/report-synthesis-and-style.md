# Report Synthesis and Style

## Purpose

Turn a confirmed MS/OR idea set into a natural referee report. The internal review may use ledgers, severity classes, counterfactuals, and audit tables; the author-facing document should sound like one informed scholar explaining a coherent judgment to other scholars.

Do not use this reference before the user confirms the substantive report plan.

## 1. Lock substance before writing

Start from the confirmed:

- paper contribution and strongest asset;
- overall publication thesis;
- retained major and secondary ideas;
- issue order and repair posture;
- recommendation label and visibility;
- verification limits.

Do not discover a new major criticism while polishing. If new evidence changes the thesis, issue set, order, or recommendation, return to the checkpoint.

## 2. Choose architecture from issue topology

Use the smallest structure that preserves the reasoning:

| Reasoning topology | Best report structure |
|---|---|
| Several independent roots | Summary → Overall Evaluation → numbered Major Comments → optional Other Comments → optional Conclusion |
| One or two spine-level roots with many manifestations | Short opening assessment → conclusion-bearing thematic headings → optional conclusion |
| Distinct judgments by Motivation, Model/Analysis, Technical Concerns, and Presentation | Dimension-led narrative; omit empty dimensions |
| One controlling point or a short journal form | Summary and Overall Assessment → Major Concern(s) → optional Minor Comments |

Do not create one section for each internal MEI dimension. MEI is a completeness lens, not necessarily the report outline. Do not mimic an AE synthesis unless the confirmed role is AE.

## 3. Write one publication thesis

The Overall Evaluation should usually do four things in one or two paragraphs:

1. identify the strongest specific asset;
2. state the central reservation as a conclusion, not a topic label;
3. explain why it controls the target-journal assessment;
4. calibrate whether repair is local, substantial but convergent, or reconstructs the paper spine.

The strongest asset is not generic courtesy. Name the theorem, formulation, mechanism, data design, algorithmic capability, or insight that remains valuable after the criticism.

If the application route and abstract theory route diverge, say so explicitly. A weak application need not erase a substantial theoretical core; a technically correct method need not inherit the importance of its application.

## 4. Build each major comment from a root chain

Before drafting, privately compress each comment to:

`root judgment -> strongest manuscript anchor/test -> paper-level consequence -> response path`

Then write connected prose:

1. **Reconstruct the claim fairly.** State what the manuscript is trying to establish and where.
2. **Perform the decisive operation.** Use the most probative comparator, counterfactual, derivation, omitted mechanism, or evidence trace.
3. **Connect details to the upper-level judgment.** Explain why those details jointly affect first-order status, novelty, validity, insight, or usefulness.
4. **State consequence and scope.** Say what readers can no longer infer and whether a narrower claim survives.
5. **Request a proportionate response.** Ask for the evidence, comparison, theorem, stress test, repositioning, or reconstruction that could change the assessment.

The labels above are hidden. Do not print “claim at risk,” “P0,” “evidence verdict,” “repair scope,” or similar audit language.

## 5. Preserve root order without becoming rigid

Order comments by control over the publication case:

- A wrong research layer or unestablished first-order object usually precedes evidence about a downstream implementation.
- An independent behavioral recourse or guarantee-semantics problem deserves its own comment when it survives repair of the first root.
- A local proof, algebra, or pseudocode error belongs later unless it breaks the main theorem or integrated contribution.
- A contingent simulation or exposition issue may move to Other Comments when rebuilding an earlier root would make it obsolete.

This is not a universal priority list. A central theorem error or a theory–implementation mismatch can control when the paper's claimed contribution depends on that exact bridge.

## 6. Calibrate language to evidence

Use direct conclusions for verified results:

- “The implemented index is not algebraically equivalent to the policy analyzed in Theorem 2.”
- “The experiment estimates an average effect, whereas the conclusion is stated at the individual level.”

Use burden-of-proof language for consequential but unresolved premises:

- “The manuscript has not yet established that this tactical lever is a first-order driver of the motivating outcome.”
- “The theoretical delta relative to the closest oracle-based method remains unclear under matched assumptions.”

Do not turn “not established” into “false.” State the concrete comparison that could resolve the issue. Avoid vague phrases such as “unrealistic assumptions”, “limited novelty”, or “more experiments are needed” without naming the mechanism and consequence.

## 7. Keep Motivation, Execution, and Insight connected

The final report need not have MEI headings, but its thesis must answer all three:

- **Motivation:** Is the exact question important, and is the focal object first-order or appropriately scoped?
- **Execution:** Does the model–proof–algorithm–evidence chain support the claim at its stated scope?
- **Insight:** What non-obvious result, capability, boundary, or decision change survives the closest baseline?

Integrate them causally. For example, an omitted upstream decision is not merely a realism concern: it can show that a rigorous algorithm solves a downstream problem and that the reported comparative statics do not explain the headline phenomenon. Conversely, a strong abstract theorem may supply a viable theory route even when the application motivation needs contraction.

## 8. Use technical detail selectively

Include equations, counterexamples, or matched comparisons when they do real argumentative work. One exact rank reversal, special-case reduction, or normalized rate comparison is often stronger than several pages of general concern.

For a technical derivation:

- define only the notation needed for the point;
- show the decisive equality, inequality, or counterexample;
- state which policy, theorem, or interpretation changes;
- avoid reproducing exploratory calculations that do not affect the conclusion.

## 9. End comments and reports cleanly

End each major comment with an outcome, not a rhetorical question. A question may introduce uncertainty, but the paragraph should say what answer or analysis is required.

Use Other/Minor Comments only for points worth the authors' time. Do not pad the report with copyediting to appear thorough.

A Conclusion is optional. Include it when it clarifies the strongest surviving asset, controlling bottleneck, and realistic path forward. Do not repeat every comment or introduce a new concern.

## 10. Final read-through

Before delivery, check:

1. Could a reader infer the overall assessment without reading the final recommendation label?
2. Does every negative upper-level judgment have a specific manuscript anchor and operation?
3. Does every major detail earn a paper-level consequence?
4. Is the first major comment the actual controlling root, unless another independent issue more directly breaks the paper?
5. Are independent issues separated and symptoms merged?
6. Are the manuscript's strongest result and surviving route stated accurately?
7. Has all internal audit machinery disappeared?
8. Does the requested response match the repair scope?
9. Are tentative claims still tentative?
10. Does the prose sound like a report rather than a rubric, scorecard, or generated issue dump?
